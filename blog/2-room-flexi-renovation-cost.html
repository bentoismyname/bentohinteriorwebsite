2-room flexi renovation: what it costs and what is worth paying for

By Benjamin Toh·10 Dec 2026·3 min read

Key points

  • Recently reported budgets for 2-room flats run from about $30,000 to $80,000, depending on how much is rebuilt.
  • Bathrooms, flooring and built-in storage drive most of the cost.
  • In a small flat, storage and lighting matter more than decorative features.
  • Seniors may qualify for subsidised safety fittings under HDB’s EASE programme.

Two-room flexi flats used to be treated as a stopgap. That has changed. In July 2026, The Sunday Times reported on seniors and downsizers spending $50,000 to $80,000 to turn these flats into homes for the long term, and I was one of the designers interviewed for the story. You can read it on Home & Decor.

I have designed at least ten 2-room flats since 2019. This is what I tell owners about cost, and about where the money does the most good.

What people are spending

These figures are as reported in that article. They are ranges from real projects, not a price list.

  • A basic 2-room renovation used to cost roughly $20,000 to $30,000.
  • Overhauling the toilet, the flooring and the built-in storage can now reach about $50,000.
  • One design firm said most of its clients spend $30,000 to $60,000.
  • My larger 2-room projects typically run from about $45,000 to $80,000.

The article also noted that some downsizers sell a larger flat first, which frees up cash, and that shorter leases for seniors lower the price of the flat itself. Both leave more room for the renovation.

Where the money goes

  • Bathroom. New tiles, waterproofing and fittings make this the most expensive room per square foot. If your flat is newly built, note that HDB has a 3-year restriction on removing bathroom wall and floor finishes, so plan around it.
  • Flooring. Replacing or overlaying the floor across the whole flat.
  • Built-in storage. Wardrobes, kitchen cabinets and full-height storage walls. In most homes, carpentry is the largest single line.
  • Electrical and lighting. Extra points, layered lighting and any smart controls.
  • Kitchen. Counters, the sink and hob, and any change to the layout.

What is worth paying for in a small flat

Storage, planned first

A flat of around 500 square feet has no spare corner. List what you own, room by room, before anything is drawn. Then build storage into the walls at full height, so the floor stays clear.

Lighting in layers

One ceiling light flattens a small room. A mix of ceiling, wall and concealed lighting gives it depth, and it lets the same room work for cooking, reading and resting.

A bathroom that is safe and easy to clean

You will use it every day for decades. Spend here before you spend on a feature wall.

One strong idea, done properly

Small homes suffer when every wall has a different feature. In Hotel-at-Home, walnut fluted panels run through the bedroom and living area, and the furniture is the focal point, which draws the eye away from the size of the room. That home received an Honorary Award at the Singapore Interior Design Awards 2022, in the category for homes of 50 sqm and below.

A small flat has no spare corner. Plan the storage before the style.

Where you can hold back

  • Decorative features that take up floor area
  • Built-ins on every wall. Leave some room to change your mind later.
  • Smart devices you are unlikely to use

If you are renovating for a senior

Design for the person ten years from now. That means slip-resistant floors, as few steps and kerbs as possible, clear walkways, handles that are easy to grip, and good light on the way to the bathroom at night.

Before you pay for safety fittings yourself, check HDB’s Enhancement for Active Seniors (EASE) programme. It offers subsidised items such as grab bars, slip-resistant treatment for bathroom floors and ramps. Singapore Citizen households can apply if a family member is 65 or older, or is aged 60 to 64 and needs help with at least one activity of daily living.

Before you ask for quotes

Decide your scope first, because scope decides the price. Then:

  • Learn to read a quotation line by line, so you can compare firms fairly.
  • Check the accreditations of anyone you shortlist. For an HDB flat, the contractor must be HDB-registered.
  • Decide whether you need a designer, a contractor or both.
